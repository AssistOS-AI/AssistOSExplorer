DPU terminology

DPU

DPU is the product identifier used by dpuAgent, the server-side authority for confidential objects, secrets, research resources, permissions, jobs, audit, and provenance. The repository does not define a longer expansion for the identifier. Explorer projects these records into file-like views, but DPU resolves the verified actor, enforces access, encrypts protected values, and owns every mutation.

Model Context Protocol

Model Context Protocol is the configured tool boundary used by the agent. Tool names and input schemas come from the local MCP configuration, and execution remains subject to verified router invocation plus DPU domain authorization.

Ploinky

Ploinky starts the agent, supplies its runtime environment, and mediates authenticated invocation context. The agent does not treat client-declared identity as authorization, and direct agent ports are not the user-facing trust boundary.

ACL

An access control list (ACL) records which user or agent principals may access, read, write, administer, or share a DPU object. ACL evaluation occurs inside DPU from verified invocation identity. Explorer may display a permission or request a confirmed change, but it cannot grant access itself.

Confidential tree

The Confidential tree contains the actor-owned My Space folder and nested objects linked by parentId. Shared, Secrets, Research Data, Jobs, and role-dependent Audit are named projections; they do not form an exhaustive list of folders that can exist beneath My Space.

Research projection

Research Data and Jobs are virtual projections over resource and job records. They expose provider, revision, policy, provenance, and execution state without representing those records as ordinary confidential folders or granting normal filesystem access to their protected content.

Materialization

A materialization is the private, revision-specific copy of provider data stored by DPU after streaming and checksum verification. Users and agents do not receive its physical path. They consume files through manifest-constrained list, stat, and bounded read operations that re-evaluate invocation scope and the resource ACL on every call.

Compute backend

A compute backend is a DPU-managed configuration for federated or secure processing. An administrator binds it to a DPU secret reference, expected deployment identity, supported capabilities, and provider-specific settings. It remains unavailable to ordinary callers until its adapter passes a connection and identity test; secure backends additionally require attestation and policy configuration.

NVFlare

NVFlare is the federated-learning runtime reached by DPU through a private Python bridge using the FLARE API. DPU controls which administrator-defined job template can be submitted, resolves participant-resource policy before confirmation, tracks the returned external job identifier, and records a successful model as a protected derived resource. NVFlare executes the distributed job while DPU remains responsible for authorization, confirmation, job projection, output release, audit, and provenance.
